# Default stance (writing)

## Author evidence comes first

- Do not invent results, mechanisms, references, methods, novelty, sample sizes, statistics, or limitations.
- Write the argument before writing the sentences.
- Reuse supplied facts and prior decisions. If missing information would materially change the scientific claim or requested deliverable, follow `workflow.md` step 3b; do not invent a premise to fill the gap.

## Reader workflow

See `../../../nature-shared/core/reader-workflow.md` (loaded via manifest `always_load`) for the 5-step reader question sequence. Draft and revise so the paper answers those questions in order.

## Claim discipline

- Use ambitious but bounded claims.
- Calibrate verbs: `show`, `demonstrate`, `suggest`, `indicate`, `enable`, `may`, `could`.
- Remove unsupported novelty and universal claims (`first ever`, `unprecedented`, `revolutionary`) unless the evidence genuinely supports them.

## Intake — required inputs before drafting

Identify before writing:

- **manuscript section**: title, abstract, introduction, related-work, method, results/experiments, discussion, conclusion, significance paragraph, or full outline
- **paper type**: research / methods / hypothesis / algorithmic / review (see paper_type fragment)
- **core claim**: what the paper actually demonstrates
- **evidence**: figures, measurements, comparisons, datasets, statistics, or examples
- **boundary**: where the claim stops
- **target venue, submission year/track, and page or word limit** if provided
- **terminology**: reuse known canonical forms and track recurring methods, models, datasets, metrics, abbreviations, and notation relevant to the requested scope. Show the ledger for a full manuscript, on request, or when important ambiguity needs resolution (see `../../../nature-shared/core/terminology-ledger.md`).

Assess these inputs against the requested scope rather than treating them as a form the user must fill out. Extract what is already present in the supplied material and use `workflow.md` step 3b for material gaps. Isolated missing facts may remain explicit placeholders in a useful draft; never present them as established evidence.
