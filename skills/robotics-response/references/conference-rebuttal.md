# Conference rebuttal workflow

Use this reference only for a conference author-response or rebuttal submitted before the
final decision. It does not govern journal revisions, post-decision appeals, or camera-ready
changes.

## Rule verification gate

Before drafting final prose, record the following from the current venue and year:

- venue, track, year, and submission platform;
- official author-response instructions or a user-supplied authoritative copy;
- opening and closing time with time zone;
- word, character, or page budget and its counting method;
- whether references, reviewer quotations, links, attachments, figures, or formatting count;
- whether manuscript edits, new experiments, supplementary material, or external links are
  permitted;
- reviewer, area-chair, or meta-reviewer visibility and anonymity rules.

Prefer the current official venue page or submission-system instructions. Do not encode limits
from memory or reuse a previous year's policy. If the rules cannot be verified, continue only
with issue triage and a clearly labelled draft. Ask for the official instructions before calling
the response compliant or submission-ready.

## Evidence and commitment rules

- Preserve reviewer and chair identifiers exactly as supplied.
- Separate work already visible in the submission, work completed and inspectable after review,
  planned work, and work that cannot be completed in the response window.
- Never turn planned work into a completed result or promise an experiment, analysis, release,
  citation, figure, or manuscript change that has not been performed and verified.
- When new work is forbidden or unavailable, answer with existing evidence, correct a factual
  misunderstanding, narrow the claim, or state the limitation.
- Do not cite private reviewer material outside the visibility boundary established by the
  official rules.

## Drafting workflow

1. Preserve the complete reviews and extract stable reviewer or chair IDs.
2. Build an internal issue ledger. Merge duplicated concerns internally while retaining every
   source ID.
3. Rank issues by decision impact: central correctness or validity, missing evidence or unfair
   comparison, misunderstood contribution, reproducibility or reporting, then local wording.
4. Allocate the verified budget across issues before drafting. Reserve enough space for every
   decision-critical concern; do not spend the opening on generic thanks or a paper summary.
5. Draft one compact author response unless the official platform requires separate fields.
   Organize it by cross-cutting issue or reviewer ID according to the interface, and keep source
   IDs visible so reviewers and chairs can map each answer back to the reviews.
6. Lead each answer with the direct resolution, then the minimum evidence and boundary needed
   to support it. Use exact manuscript sections, tables, figures, or supplied results only when
   they are inspectable.
7. Handle disagreements narrowly and professionally. State the evidence and, when warranted,
   calibrate the claim rather than attacking reviewer competence.
8. Count the final response using the venue's stated method. Report the measured count and leave
   a visible margin when the platform's counter may differ.

Do not generate a journal revision cover letter, red-marked manuscript, reviewer-private files,
or a Major/Minor Revision strategy unless the verified conference rules explicitly require one.
